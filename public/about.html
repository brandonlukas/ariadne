ariadne. how it works

A thread through the literature

What ariadne actually does, told the way it happens.

Your PI drops two papers in Slack — say, Restormer, a famous image-restoration transformer, and a niche paper on denoising retinal OCT scans — and says get familiar with this space. What you need isn't one reading list. It's four: the groundwork everything rests on, the seeds' contemporaries, where the field went next, and what's happening right now. A single ranking can't be all four at once — most tools pretend otherwise, and hand you whatever "related papers" means that day. Ariadne makes each question its own, inspectable answer.

what a weave gathers

Paste your papers as seeds and weave. For each seed, ariadne pulls every paper it cited — a bibliography is small, so nothing is sampled — and then three deliberate 50-paper slices of the papers citing it: the all-time most-cited, the newest, and the last two years ranked by traction. That third slice exists because "most cited" is a contest old papers always win; without it, the recent work you most need never enters the room.

from the example weave — Restormer's bibliography alone carries its rivals: SwinIR, MPRNet, IPT, Uformer. Its top-cited citers bring HAT and CDDFuse; the frontier slice brings MambaIR (2024); the newest slice reaches papers published this year with single-digit citations.

Then the trick that costs nothing: every fetched citer carries its own reference list, so ariadne counts how often each paper in the wider literature is cited alongside your seed. Papers that keep appearing next to it — co-cited by a tenth of its sampled citers — are admitted too. This is the only route to a seed's true peers: concurrent work can't cite your seed and was never cited by it, so no direct edge exists. When later papers keep citing the two together, the field has already decided they belong side by side. Ariadne listens.

the proof — MAXIM, Restormer's head-to-head CVPR 2022 competitor, neither cites Restormer nor is cited by it. It enters the weave purely because Restormer's citers keep citing the two together.

And then — nothing is cut. Everything the harvest gathers stays in the weave. There used to be a 200-paper cap here; it turned out to be a layout budget masquerading as curation, so it's gone. The admission rules above — complete bibliographies, deliberate samples, relative co-citation bars — are the curation, and the rankings decide what you read first. Read the top ten or walk all four hundred; the corpus doesn't presume to know where you'll stop.

the rarest papers — exactly three papers in the example weave (401 papers, ~4,000 edges) directly touch both seeds: U-Net, DnCNN, and StruNet, a 24-citation 2023 transformer for medical-image denoising that is arguably the single most relevant paper in the corpus. They carry a bridges badge, and the ⋈ filter isolates them in one click.

the weave has a direction

One row of plain questions drives everything — ask: canon · groundwork · since · now — and each question silently chooses a harvest direction, a weighting, and an order. groundwork weaves backward: every reference, and — counted from data already in hand — the references of the references, the canon behind the canon. Ancestry is a closed world (a 2022 paper's past is finished), so that weave is durable and all recency machinery switches off; it ranks by a personalized PageRank (a reader starts at your seeds and forever follows references — where do they keep arriving?), and a story toggle reads it oldest-first. since and now weave forward — citer samples, co-cited peers, the frontier — ranked by momentum or by date. canon weaves both directions into one graph: the only place bridges and the two-lobe constellation can exist. Each direction is woven once and cached; the link carries the question.

roots, measured — for the example seeds, roots spans 1991–2023 with 0% from the last three years (ancestry is finished), and its era rank opens with Optical Coherence Tomography (Huang et al., 1991, Science) — the founding paper of the OCT seed's entire field, two hops back. Load-bearing reads like a qualifying exam: U-Net, DnCNN, ResNet, BM3D, AlexNet, non-local means, K-SVD.

four scores, in the open

Every paper in the weave gets four scores, each visible as a meter when you open it. Nothing is hidden behind a similarity number.

foundationalPageRank

The algorithm that made early Google, run on your corpus's citation graph. Imagine a reader who forever follows references backward from random starting points: the papers they keep arriving at are the ones the rest of the literature structurally rests on. In the example weave: U-Net — half the corpus ultimately routes back to it.

bridgebetweenness centrality

How often a paper sits on the shortest path between two other papers in the corpus. High scores mark connectors — the papers that join otherwise-separate conversations, like the method paper linking a computer-vision cluster to a medical-imaging one. In the example weave: DnCNN, the denoiser both the vision lobe and the OCT lobe route through.

momentumcitation velocity

Recent citations, tempered two ways: log-damped so one blockbuster doesn't flatten everyone else, and blended with the fraction of a paper's lifetime citations earned in the last three years — high for new-and-rising work at any absolute size, near zero for a settled classic still coasting on inertia. In the example weave: MambaIR — 447 citations, essentially all of them from the last two years.

relevanceseed proximity

How few citation hops separate the paper from your seeds — with a boost for papers touching several seeds at once, because proximity alone can't tell a paper that cites one seed from the far rarer paper that cites them all. In the example weave: StruNet, which cites both seeds and outscores papers a hundred times more famous on this axis.

Raw values of these things are wildly skewed — one paper with 90,000 citations would compress everyone else to zero — so each score is rank-normalized: what matters is a paper's position among its 200 peers, not its absolute magnitude.

one weighting would be a lie

Each question blends the four scores differently, and the weights are the point. canon leans on foundational and bridge — the committee-exam list. since leans on momentum and relevance, and applies an era gate: papers published at or before your latest seed are discounted, because a 2015 paper cannot be a development since 2022 no matter how many citations it still collects — this single rule is what keeps U-Net from topping every list forever. now is honest chronology over the forward weave. And the seeds' own years are marked with red ticks on the year histogram, so brushing across them turns canon into a fifth view: the contemporaries — the papers your seeds competed with, recovered from their bibliographies and their co-citations.

the same corpus, three answers — canon's top non-seed papers: U-Net (2015), DnCNN (2017), ResNet (2016). Since's: HAT, CDDFuse, and the diffusion-based weather-restoration line — entirely 2023 and later, because the era gate holds. Now leads with 2025–2026 work the traction contest would have discarded. One list pretending to answer all three would be lying about at least two.

This is the quiet difference from the usual "related papers" box: every ranking here is a legible formula over four visible scores, steerable by intent, with its biases named and gated — not a similarity score you have to take on faith.

the ai thread, if you want it

Add a free OpenRouter API key (it stays in your browser, and a "turn off" control forgets it along with everything it produced) and ariadne reads your seed set once and infers the question you're actually asking. From then on, opening any paper generates a two-sentence verdict against your question — and it is allowed to say no: a famous paper that doesn't serve your thread gets told so. The status chip always shows which model answered.

and then your afternoon

Skim canon and star what you haven't read. Switch to catch-up for what happened since. Brush the seed ticks for the contemporaries. Click ⋈ bridges for the rare papers touching all your seeds — often the closest published ancestors of your own project. Hide the noise (bibliographic databases have plenty; hidden papers leave every view and every export). Promote your best find to a seed and reweave. Export BibTeX into your reference manager, and send the URL to your PI — the whole weave lives in the link and rebuilds itself on their machine.
